# Lean development plan, milestone 15: Pyber's CFSG-free Lemma 4.1

Prepared 26 October 2026. This continues `PLAN-14.md` (the main recursion, its cost and the
graph-isomorphism entry; milestone 14 is complete, `80bea6c`). The conventions and standards of
`PLAN.md` and `lean/docs/` remain in force.

## Goal

Discharge the interface `AltQuotientIso K` of `PLAN-12.md` §1 without CFSG, with an explicit
threshold that meets the growth conditions of `QPHyp` (`PLAN-14.md` §14.13):

```
Kpy n = 192 (λ(n) + 1)⁵,   λ = ⌊log₂⌋ = Nat.log 2.
```

`Kpy` is monotone and `Kpy n ≤ 192 (λ(n) + 1)⁵`. After this milestone `QPHyp Kpy T 192 C'`
holds for every `T` with `TransitivityBound T` and `T m ≤ C' (λ(m) + 1)`, so the headline
theorems of milestone 14 depend only on Wielandt's bound (milestone 16).

The source is László Pyber, *A CFSG-free analysis of Babai's quasipolynomial GI algorithm*,
arXiv:1605.08266: Theorem 7 (point stabilizers of transitive groups
have few abelian composition factors) and Lemma 12 (the weak Lemma 4.1).

## 1. The route, and where it departs from Pyber

Throughout, `n` is the degree and `λ = ⌊log₂⌋`. Groups are finite.

- **Abelian composition factors.** `compA G` is the set of primes `p` for which `G` has
  subgroups `B ≤ A` with `A` subnormal (mathlib's `Subgroup.IsSubnormal`), `B` normal in `A` and
  `[A : B] = p`. By Schreier refinement and Jordan–Hölder these are exactly the orders of the
  abelian composition factors (Pyber's `Comp_A`), but nothing below uses that equivalence: every
  property is proved from this definition. No composition series is formalized.
- **Point stabilizers, abstractly.** "A point stabilizer of a transitive group of degree `n`" is
  a subgroup `H ≤ G` with trivial normal core and index `n` (the action on `G ⧸ H`). The
  reductions of Pyber's Lemma 8 are proved in this form, and the primitive case in the concrete
  form `G ≤ Sym(Ω)`, with bridges between the two.
- **Explicit constants.** Theorem 7 becomes `|compA H| ≤ 2 λ(n)² + 1`. The claim in Pyber's
  Lemma 2 becomes `ω(|G|) ≤ λ(n) (λ(n) + 1) / 2` for transitive groups of odd order, `ω` being
  the number of distinct prime factors. Proposition 3 becomes
  `compA G ∖ compA H ⊆ primeFactors [G : H]`, with `ω(m) ≤ λ(m)`.
- **Wielandt's Lemma 4, self-paired form.** Lemma 10 applies Lemma 4 only to a self-paired
  suborbit, and that is the only form proved. Wielandt's smallest subnormal subgroup `H*` is
  taken for the class of groups `Q` with `compA Q ⊆ S`, a residual over chains of normal steps.
  This class is closed under subnormal subgroups, quotients and extensions, which is all the
  argument uses.
- **Lemma 10 without the socle theory of Proposition 9.** Let `P` be primitive of degree `d` and
  `N` a minimal normal subgroup of `P`:
  - if `N` is abelian, it is regular, and a point stabilizer `P_y` acts faithfully on a
    generating list of `N` of length at most `λ(d)` by conjugation, so `|P| ≤ d^{λ(d) + 1}`.
    This replaces `P / Soc(P) ↪ GL(s, p)`;
  - if `N` is nonabelian, it is characteristically simple, so `compA N = ∅` (by induction on
    `|N|`), and `compA P ⊆ compA P_y` because `P = N P_y`.
- **Any self-paired suborbit** serves in Lemma 10 (Pyber takes the smallest one, but his proof
  does not use minimality). An involution gives one.
- **Jordan's Lemma 1** (needed for odd order) through the orbital digraph. The fixed points of
  an element of prime order `p ∤ |G_x^Δ|` form a set closed along the orbital, hence along its
  reverse (follow the powers of an element mapping one end of an edge to the other). By
  primitivity, the connected component of `x` is everything.
- **Proposition 11 is replaced by Chebyshev's bound in mathlib**, in its natural-number form
  `2^N ≤ (N + 1) lcm(1, …, N)` (`Chebyshev.two_pow_le_mul_lcmUpto`) with
  `lcm(1, …, N) ≤ N^{π(N)}` (from `Nat.lcmUpto_eq_prod_pow_log`); the real-valued
  `Chebyshev.pi_ge` is not needed (decided in stage 15d). It gives
  `π(64 L³) ≥ 2 L² + 1` for `L ≥ 1`. So with `L = λ(n) + 1` there are `2 L² − 3 L + 3`
  distinct odd primes, at most `64 L³` each, whose sum is at most `192 L⁵`.
- **Lemma 12's cyclic subgroup.** It is generated by a permutation whose cycle type is those
  primes (mathlib's `Equiv.Perm.exists_with_cycleType_iff`). It is even because the cycle
  lengths are odd (`sign_of_cycleType`), and each of them divides its order
  (`dvd_of_mem_cycleType`).

**Lemma 12, assembled.** Let `G` be primitive of degree `n`, `φ : G → Alt(Λ)` onto, and
`k = |Λ| ≥ Kpy n`. Suppose `N = ker φ ≠ 1`. Then `N` is transitive, and so is
`H = φ⁻¹(⟨σ⟩)`, for the permutation `σ` above. `compA H` contains the
`R = 2 λ² + λ + 2` primes of `σ`'s order. By Proposition 3 and Theorem 7 it has at most
`2 λ² + 1 + λ` elements, a contradiction.

## 3. Stage 15a: completion criteria (the group theory of `compA`)

`GraphIso/Pyber/CompA.lean`, and further files in `GraphIso/Pyber/` as convenient, with
mathlib imports only. `G` is a finite group throughout, and statements about `↥A ⧸ B` take `B`
normal in `A`.

1. **The definition.** `compA G : Set ℕ` as in §1. The docstring states the definition exactly,
   and says that its equivalence with the abelian composition factors (Schreier refinement and
   Jordan–Hölder) is neither proved nor used.
2. **Basic properties.**
   - `compA G ⊆ (Nat.card G).primeFactors`. Hence `(compA G).ncard ≤ ω(|G|) ≤ λ(|G|)`, with
     `(m.primeFactors).card ≤ Nat.log 2 m` proved for every `m`.
   - **Images:** for a surjective homomorphism `f : G →* G'`, `compA G' ⊆ compA G`. Isomorphic
     groups have equal `compA`.
   - **Subnormal subgroups:** if `H` is subnormal (in particular normal), then
     `compA H ⊆ compA G`.
   - **Extensions:** if `N` is normal, then `compA G ⊆ compA N ∪ compA (G ⧸ N)`.
   - **Prime index:** if `N` is normal of prime index `p`, then `p ∈ compA G`. If `G` is
     commutative and `p` is a prime dividing `|G|`, then `p ∈ compA G`.
   - **Simple groups:** a nonabelian simple group has `compA G = ∅`.
3. **Proposition 3 (index).** For every subgroup `H ≤ G`,
   `compA G ∖ compA H ⊆ H.index.primeFactors`. Proof: for `A` subnormal, `[A : A ∩ H]` divides
   `[G : H]`, by induction along `IsSubnormal`. And if `B ⊴ A` has prime index `p ∤ [G : H]`,
   then `[A ∩ H : B ∩ H] = p`.
4. **Families of normal subgroups.** Let `(N i)` be a finite family of normal subgroups.
   - If `⨅ i, N i = ⊥`, then `compA G ⊆ ⋃ i, compA (G ⧸ N i)` (Pyber's Proposition 6 for
     subdirect products).
   - If `⨆ i, N i = ⊤`, then `compA G ⊆ ⋃ i, compA (N i)`.
5. **Characteristically simple groups.** If `N` is nontrivial and noncommutative, and every
   characteristic subgroup of `N` is `⊥` or `⊤`, then `compA N = ∅`. Proof by induction on
   `|N|`:
   - take a minimal normal subgroup `M` of `N`; it is characteristically simple;
   - if `M` is abelian, the join of the images of `M` under the automorphisms of `N` is
     characteristic and solvable, so `N` is solvable. Then `N' < N` is characteristic, so
     `N' = ⊥`, a contradiction;
   - otherwise, if `M = N`, then `N` is simple; else `compA M = ∅` by induction, and item 4
     (joins) applies to the images of `M`.

   Also: a minimal normal subgroup of a finite group is characteristically simple, as a group.
6. **Residuals** (Wielandt's `H*` for the class `compA ⊆ S`), for subgroups of `G` and a set
   `S` of primes.
   - `SChain S A L` holds if there is a chain `A = A₀ ≥ A₁ ≥ … ≥ A_r = L` in which each `A_{i+1}`
     is normal in `A_i` and `compA (A_i ⧸ A_{i+1}) ⊆ S`.
   - `resid S A` is the infimum of all `L` with `SChain S A L`. It is defined without choice.
   - **It is reached:** `SChain S A (resid S A)`. The reachable subgroups are closed under
     intersection, since intersecting a chain with a subnormal subgroup gives subnormal
     sections.
   - **It is conjugation-equivariant:** `resid S (g A g⁻¹) = g (resid S A) g⁻¹`. Hence
     anything that normalizes `A` normalizes `resid S A`.
   - **One step does not change it:** if `B` is normal in `A` and `compA (A ⧸ B) ⊆ S`, then
     `resid S A = resid S B`.
   - **Its use:** `SChain S A L → compA A ⊆ S ∪ compA L`. In particular
     `resid S A = ⊥ → compA A ⊆ S`.
7. **Short generating lists.** Every finite group has a list of at most `λ(|G|)` elements that
   generates it. Each new element at least doubles the subgroup generated.
8. **Checks** (`GraphIsoChecks/Pyber.lean`). The milestone is theory, as milestones 6, 8 and 12
   were. The checks are instantiations, with kernel `decide` for the numbers:
   - `compA (Perm (Fin 3)) = {2, 3}`, and `compA` of the cyclic group of order 12 is `{2, 3}`;
   - a negative control: `compA (alternatingGroup (Fin 5)) = ∅`, although
     `60.primeFactors = {2, 3, 5}`. So `compA` is not the set of prime divisors;
   - Proposition 3 on `Alt(3) ≤ Sym(3)` (index 2), where `3 ∈ compA` of both;
   - the residual in `Sym(3)`: `resid {2, 3} ⊤ = ⊥`. For `S = {2}`, `resid S ⊤ ≠ ⊥`, by item 6,
     since `3 ∈ compA (Perm (Fin 3))`;
   - numerical facts: `(m.primeFactors).card ≤ Nat.log 2 m` for `m < 65`, by kernel `decide`.
9. **Audit and docs.**
   - Every new declaration is in AxiomAudit, and the new files are in its import closure.
   - `theorem-map.md` and `status.md` get a 15a section, stating exactly what is proved.
   - The principal results are protected statements of the loop: Proposition 3, the
     extension lemma, the family lemmas, the characteristically simple theorem and the
     residual's use.

**Not in this stage:** permutation groups (15b), Theorem 7 (15c) and primes (15d).

## 4. Stage 15b: completion criteria (permutation groups and the odd-order bound)

Stage 15a is done (`f877fa3`, approved after one audit with no findings). This stage proves the
permutation-group lemmas behind Pyber's Lemmas 1–2, and the reductions that Lemma 8 (stage 15c)
reuses. The files are in `GraphIso/Pyber/`, with mathlib imports only. Wielandt's Lemma 4 moves
to stage 15c (§2).

Two settings appear. The **abstract** one is a finite group `G` with a subgroup `H` such that
`H.normalCore = ⊥` (a *core-free* subgroup), of index `n`. The **concrete** one is a group `G`
acting on `Ω`, faithfully: `Faithful G Ω` says that only `1` fixes every point. No new notions
of orbital or suborbit are defined. The orbital of `(x, y)` is `orbit G (x, y)` for the
diagonal action on `Ω × Ω`, and the suborbit is `Δ(x) = orbit (stabilizer G x) y`.

1. **Bridges.**
   - **Concrete to abstract:** for a faithful transitive action and every `x`,
     `stabilizer G x` is core-free; its index is `|Ω|` (mathlib's
     `index_stabilizer_of_transitive`). A subgroup of `Perm Ω` acts faithfully, and the image of
     a transitive action in `Perm Ω` acts transitively.
   - **Abstract to concrete:** for `H` core-free, `G` acts faithfully and transitively on
     `G ⧸ H`, and the stabilizer of the base coset is `H` (mathlib's `stabilizer_quotient`). If
     `H ≠ ⊤`, then `G ⧸ H` has two points; if `H` is maximal (`IsCoatom H`), the action is
     primitive (mathlib's `isCoatom_stabilizer_iff_preprimitive`).
2. **Reductions through an intermediate subgroup.**
   - **The quotient by the core:** for every subgroup `L ≤ G`, the image of `L` in
     `G ⧸ L.normalCore` is core-free of index `[G : L]`. It is applied to `M` in `G`, and to
     `H ∩ K` in `K`.
   - **Normal subgroups:** let `H` be core-free, `K` normal, and `J` the core of `H ∩ K` in `K`.
     Then every prime dividing `|K|` divides `|K ⧸ J|` (an element of prime order in `K` has a
     conjugate outside `H`), and `compA K ⊆ compA (K ⧸ J)` (the conjugates of `J` under `G`
     intersect trivially, and each quotient is isomorphic to `K ⧸ J`; 15a item 4).
   - **Covering subgroups:** if `M` covers `H` (`H ⋖ M`), `K ≤ M` is normal and `K ≠ ⊥`, then
     `K ⊔ H = M`, and then `[K : H ∩ K] = [M : H]` (from 15a's `relIndex_eq_mul_relIndex`). If
     `H` is neither `⊤` nor maximal, some `M ≠ ⊤` covers `H`.
3. **Orbitals.** For `x ≠ y`:
   - `(x, b)` lies in the orbital iff `b ∈ Δ(x)`, and `|Δ(x)| = [G_x : G_x ∩ G_y]`;
   - **paired suborbits:** for a transitive action of a finite group on a finite set, the
     paired suborbit `{b | (b, x) ∈ orbit G (x, y)}` has the size of `Δ(x)`;
   - **odd order:** if `|G|` is odd, no orbital is self-paired, `(y, x) ∉ orbit G (x, y)`, and
     (for a transitive action on a finite set) `2 |Δ(x)| + 1 ≤ |Ω|`;
   - **involutions:** if `g * g = 1`, then `(g • x, x) ∈ orbit G (x, g • x)`.
4. **Connectivity.** For a primitive action of a finite group, `x ≠ y`, and `F ∋ x` closed along
   the orbital (`a ∈ F` and `(a, b) ∈ orbit G (x, y)` imply `b ∈ F`): `F = Ω`. Closure along the
   orbital implies closure along its reverse, by following the powers of an element mapping
   one end of an edge to the other. The class of `x` under the generated equivalence is then a
   block containing `x` and `y`.
5. **Jordan's Lemma 1.** For a faithful primitive action of a finite group, `x ≠ y`, and `P` the
   image of `G_x` in `Perm Δ(x)`: every prime dividing `|G_x|` divides `|P|`. An element of
   prime order `p ∤ |P|` acts trivially on every suborbit at a point it fixes, so its fixed
   points are closed along the orbital, and by item 4 it is trivial.
6. **The odd-order bound** (the claim in Pyber's Lemma 2): for `G : Type` finite of odd order and
   `H` core-free of index `n`, `ω(|G|) ≤ λ(n) (λ(n) + 1) / 2`, and the same for a faithful
   transitive action of degree `n`. By strong induction on `n`:
   - `H = ⊤`: `G` is trivial;
   - `H` maximal: the action on `G ⧸ H` is primitive and faithful. For a suborbit `Δ` of size
     `d`, `2 d + 1 ≤ n` (item 3), and `P ≤ Perm Δ` is transitive of odd order. Item 5 gives
     `ω(|G|) ≤ ω(n) + ω(|P|) ≤ λ(n) + λ(d)(λ(d) + 1)/2`, with `λ(d) + 1 ≤ λ(n)`;
   - otherwise take `H < M < G` and `K = M.normalCore`. Item 2 gives
     `ω(|G|) ≤ ω(|G ⧸ K|) + ω(|K ⧸ J|) ≤ T(λ(t)) + T(λ(b))` with `T(L) = L(L+1)/2`,
     `t = [G : M]`, `b = [M : H]`, `t b = n` and `t, b ≥ 2`. No minimality of `M` is needed,
     since `[K : H ∩ K] ≤ [M : H]`.
7. **Checks** (`GraphIsoChecks/PyberPerm.lean`):
   - `Sym(3)` on `Fin 3`: the orbital of `(0, 1)` is self-paired, and `Δ(0) = {1, 2}`. So
     `2 |Δ(0)| + 1 = 5 > 3`, a negative control for the odd-order hypothesis. Jordan's lemma
     instantiated there: `2 ∣ |G_0| = 2`, hence `2 ∣ |P|`;
   - `Alt(3)` on `Fin 3` (order `3`): the orbital of `(0, 1)` is not self-paired,
     `2 |Δ(0)| + 1 ≤ 3`, and the odd-order bound instantiated there;
   - a negative control for connectivity: the rotations of `Fin 4` act transitively, and
     `{0, 2}` is closed along the orbital of `(0, 2)` but is not everything;
   - numbers: the two inequalities of item 6, for `n < 64` and for `t, b < 24`, by kernel
     `decide`.
8. **Audit and docs**, as for 15a: AxiomAudit, `theorem-map.md`, `status.md`. The targets are
   the odd-order bound (both forms), Jordan's lemma, the connectivity lemma, the suborbit bound,
   the bridges, and the reductions of item 2.

**Not in this stage:** Wielandt's Lemma 4 and Theorem 7 (15c), primes (15d).

## 5. Stage 15c: completion criteria (Theorem 7)

Stage 15b is done (`01b0d5c`, approved after one audit with no findings). This stage proves
Pyber's Theorem 7 with the explicit bound `|compA H| ≤ 2 λ(n)² + 1` for a core-free subgroup `H`
of index `n`. The files are `GraphIso/Pyber/{Wielandt,MinNormal,Imprimitive,Theorem7}.lean`, with
mathlib imports only. The groups of the inductions are in `Type`.

1. **Wielandt's Lemma 4, self-paired form** (`Wielandt.lean`). `G` finite, acting faithfully and
   primitively on `Ω`; `x ≠ y` with `(y, x) ∈ orbit G (x, y)`. For a point `a`, `T(a)`
   (`suborbitKer G x y a`) is the subgroup fixing `a` and every `b` with
   `(a, b) ∈ orbit G (x, y)` (a `fixingSubgroup`).
   - `T(a)` is normal in `G_a ∩ G_b` for `(a, b)` in the orbital, and
     `T(g • a) = g T(a) g⁻¹`, `G_{g a} ∩ G_{g b} = g (G_a ∩ G_b) g⁻¹`.
   - A chain does not change the residual: `SChain S A L → resid S A = resid S L`.
   - **The lemma** (`compA_suborbitKer_subset`): `compA T(x) ⊆ compA (G_{xy} ⧸ T(x))`. With `S`
     the right side and `R(a)` the residual of `T(a)`: `G_{xy} ≥ T(x)` and `G_{xy} ≥ T(y)` are
     one-step chains (the second by conjugating with an element exchanging `x` and `y`), so
     `R(x) = R(y)`; by equivariance `R(a) = R(b)` along the orbital; by the connectivity lemma
     `R(a) = R(x)` for all `a`; so `R(x)` fixes every point and is `⊥`.
   - **The corollary** (Pyber's `(∗)`, `compA_stabilizer_subset`): with `P` the image of `G_x` in
     `Perm Δ(x)` and `P_y` the stabilizer of `y` in `P`, `compA G_x ⊆ compA P ∪ compA P_y`:
     `T(x)` is the kernel of `G_x → P`, and `G_{xy} ⧸ T(x)` maps onto `P_y`.
2. **Minimal normal subgroups of primitive groups** (`MinNormal.lean`; Lemma 10 without socles).
   `G` finite, faithful and primitive on `Ω`:
   - a nontrivial normal subgroup is transitive (mathlib's quasiprimitivity);
   - a commutative transitive subgroup acts regularly, so `|N| = |Ω|`;
   - an element of `G_x` is determined by its conjugates of a generating list of a transitive
     normal `N`; so for `Ω` finite and `N` commutative, normal and nontrivial,
     `|G| ≤ |Ω|^(λ(|Ω|) + 1)` (`card_le_of_comm`);
   - for `N` minimal normal and noncommutative, `compA G ⊆ compA G_x`
     (`compA_subset_of_noncomm`): `compA N = ∅` (15a), and `G_x` maps onto `G ⧸ N`;
   - **the dichotomy** (`card_le_or_compA_subset`): for `G` nontrivial and `Ω` finite,
     `|G| ≤ |Ω|^(λ(|Ω|) + 1)` or `compA G ⊆ compA G_x`.
3. **Lemma 8, abstract form** (`Imprimitive.lean`).
   - For every subgroup `L` of a finite group `A`, `compA L ⊆ compA A ∪ compA L'`, with `L'` the
     image of `L` in `A ⧸ L.normalCore` (`compA_subset_union_core`).
   - **The lemma** (`exists_ncard_compA_le`): for any `F : ℕ → ℕ`, if every core-free subgroup
     of index `m < n` (of a finite group in `Type`) has `|compA| ≤ F m`, then every core-free
     `H` of index `n`, neither `⊤` nor maximal, has `|compA H| ≤ F t + F b + λ(b)` for some
     `t, b ≥ 2` with `t b = n`. Take `M ≠ ⊤` covering `H` and `K = M.normalCore`: if `K = ⊥`,
     `|compA H| ≤ F t + F b`; otherwise `K ⊔ H = M`, `H` maps onto `M ⧸ K` with kernel `K ∩ H`,
     and `compA (K ∩ H) ⊆ compA L' ∪ primeFactors b` by stage 15b and Proposition 3.
4. **Theorem 7** (`Theorem7.lean`). For a finite group `G : Type` and `H` core-free of index `n`,
   `|compA H| ≤ 2 λ(n)² + 1` (`ncard_compA_le_of_coreFree`). By strong induction on `n`, with
   `F(m) = 2 λ(m)² + 1`:
   - `H = ⊤`; `H` neither `⊤` nor maximal (item 3, `F t + F b + λ(b) ≤ F(n)`);
   - `H` maximal, `|G|` odd: `|compA H| ≤ ω(|G|) ≤ λ(n)(λ(n)+1)/2` (stage 15b);
   - `H` maximal, `|G|` even: an involution gives a self-paired orbital `(x, y)` on `G ⧸ H`, and
     item 1 gives `compA H ⊆ compA P ∪ compA P_y` with `P` of degree `d < n`. If `P` is trivial,
     `compA H = ∅`. If `P` is primitive, item 2: either `|P| ≤ d^(λ(d)+1)`, so
     `|compA H| ≤ ω(|P|) ≤ λ(d^(λ(d)+1)) ≤ F(n)`, or `compA H ⊆ compA P_y`, bounded by `F(d)`.
     If `P` is imprimitive, item 3 for `P_y` in `P` and Proposition 3 give
     `F t + F b + λ(b) + λ(d)` with `t b = d`.
   - The arithmetic: `F t + F b + λ(b) ≤ F(n)` for `λ(t), λ(b) ≥ 1`, `λ(t) + λ(b) ≤ λ(n)`; and
     with an extra `+ s` for `λ(t) + λ(b) ≤ s ≤ λ(n)`; `λ(d^(λ(d)+1)) ≤ 2 L² + 1` for
     `λ(d) ≤ L`.
   - Concretely, for a faithful transitive action of degree `n`: `|compA G_x| ≤ 2 λ(n)² + 1`
     (`ncard_compA_stabilizer_le`), and `|compA G| ≤ 2 λ(n)² + λ(n) + 1`
     (`ncard_compA_le_of_transitive`, `Ω` nonempty).
5. **Checks** (`GraphIsoChecks/PyberThm7.lean`):
   - Wielandt's lemma and its corollary on `Sym(3)` at the self-paired orbital of `(0, 1)`;
   - Theorem 7 on `Sym(4)`: `|compA G_0| ≤ 9` and `|compA G| ≤ 11`;
   - a negative control for core-freeness: in `Sym(3)`, `⊤` has index `1` and
     `|compA ⊤| = 2 > 2 λ(1)² + 1`;
   - the dichotomy, both alternatives: `Alt(3)` on `Fin 3` (first, `3 ≤ 9`), and `Sym(8)` on

## 7. Design notes

- **Why not composition series.** mathlib's Jordan–Hölder is stated for lattices with a
  "Jordan–Hölder lattice" structure, which subgroups do not carry directly. The existential
  definition makes every property (images, extensions, index, families) a statement about
  subnormal pairs, with no series at all.
- **Exponents.** The exponent `5` in `Kpy` is forced. Theorem 7's quadratic bound and Lemma 12's
  `√(k / log k)` primes give `k ≈ (log n)⁴ log log n`, and `QPHyp` allows `(log n)⁵`. A weaker
  Theorem 7 (for example cubic) would not fit. So the induction must keep Pyber's quadratic
  bound exactly: no additive term in the primitive case with a nonabelian minimal normal
  subgroup, and only `λ(m) + λ(d)` in the imprimitive cases.
- **Universes.** As in milestone 12, statements quantify over groups and sets in `Type`. The
  inductions pass to quotient groups and subgroups, which stay in `Type`.
