# Work remaining beyond the polynomial backend

Milestone 1 (`PLAN.md`) proved the string-isomorphism reductions and the solver against an
abstract group backend, then discharged the backend with a reference implementation that
represents each group by all its elements. Milestone 2 (`PLAN-2.md`) added the Schreier–Sims
backend, whose charges are polynomial in the degree. Milestone 3 (`PLAN-3.md`) added Luks's
strategy, polynomial for groups with bounded primitive sections. Milestones 4–9 added graph
isomorphism of bounded valence, milestones 10–16 Babai's quasipolynomial algorithm, without
CFSG, and milestone 17 runs it on Turing machines.

This list records:

- what that backend supplies;
- what it does not give by itself, and where the later milestones supply it;
- the refinements and the cost-model work that remain.

`backend-contracts.md` has the contracts and both backends' charges, `theorem-map.md` the
theorems and `status.md` the stages.

## What the Schreier–Sims backend supplies

The artefacts the theorems take, all discharged in `GraphIso/Sims/` and
`GraphIso/Solver/Sims.lean`:

1. **Data.** None (`D = Ty.unit`, `ssDV` trivially true). Each operation builds the complete
   Sims table it needs from the generators.
2. **Correct implementations.**
   - `ssOracle_meets` proves the contracts of all eight operations.
   - `ssImpl_implements` proves that the programs compute the oracle within `ssB`.
   - `ssImpl_wf` proves them well-formed.
3. **Solver bounds**: `ssBounds n G₀ : BackendBounds ssCtx ssDV n (n * n) G₀`, for every
   ambient group, with charges independent of its order. Output groups are generated by
   table entries, so `generate` compresses to at most `n²` generators without a separate
   filter.
4. **Reduction bounds**: `restrict_sims` and `blockReduce_sims` discharge every charge
   hypothesis of the two reductions.

In closed form:

- `simsQ n ≤ 400 (n + 1)⁶` (`simsQ_le`);
- `genCost n L ≤ 100 (n + 1)⁶ + 60 L (n + 1)²` (`genCost_le`);
- the solver costs at most `13 + 100 (n+1)⁶ + 60 L (n+1)² + 800 (m+1) N (n+1)⁶`
  (`simsCost_le`).

## What a polynomial backend alone does not give

- **The default strategy is exponential.** The solver's bound
  `solveBound Q m N = 2 m N Q - Q` is linear in the group order `N`. The default strategy
  branches over point stabilizers, and its recursion tree can have about `m N` nodes. With
  `Q = O(n⁶)` the bound is polynomial in `N`, which is still exponential in `n` for large
  groups. The interpreter runs show this:
  - `generate` on `M₁₁` costs about two million units;
  - a solver run on `S₆` costs about twelve million.
- **Luks's strategy** (`PLAN-3.md`, `GraphIso/Luks/`) removes the dependence on `N`. Its
  bound is `luksBound Q c m = 2 Q m ^ (c + 1) − Q` for windows of size `m ≥ 1` (`Q` for the
  empty window) whenever every primitive section of degree `k` has order at most `k ^ c`
  (`PrimBounded`):
  - proved for **nilpotent groups** with `c = 1` (`primBounded_of_isNilpotent`), giving
    `solve_luks_nilpotent`: cost at most `6640 (n + 1)⁸ + 60 L (n + 1)²`;
  - proved for **solvable groups whose order has at most `s + 1` prime divisors** with
    `c = 12 s + 1` (`primBounded_of_isSolvable`, milestone 6), giving `solve_luks_solvable`:
    cost at most `6640 (n + 1)^{36 s + 8} + 60 L (n + 1)²`;
  - proved for **groups whose simple sections have order at most `k`** with
    `c = 3 k³ + 3 k² + 12 k + 1` (`primBounded_of_boundedSections`, milestone 8, without
    CFSG), giving `solve_luks_bounded`;
  - proved for every group with `c = n` (`primBounded_degree`), an exponential bound;
  - **not proved in the form of the Babai–Cameron–Pálfy theorem for `Γ_d`**: a primitive
    group of degree `k` in `Γ_d` has order at most `k ^ O(d)`, where `Γ_d` allows abelian
    sections of every prime order (milestone 8 bounds all simple sections). Given it as a
    hypothesis, `solve_luks` applies unchanged.
- **Babai's quasipolynomial algorithm** is formalized, without CFSG (milestones 10–16):
  coherent configurations and the design lemma (`GraphIso/Coherent/`, `design_lemma`),
  Split-or-Johnson (`GraphIso/SplitJohnson/`, `split_or_johnson`), local certificates and
  their aggregation (`GraphIso/Certificates/`, `cert_full`), the recursion with its costed
  programs and its potential (`GraphIso/Main/`, `main_pot`, `mainSolve_qp_exp`), the reduction
  from graphs to strings on `V ⊔ V²` (`giEntry`, `GraphIso/Main/GraphEntryProg.lean`), Pyber's
  bound for alternating quotients (`altQuotientIso_pyber`) and a transitivity bound
  (`transitivityBound_quad`). The endpoints have no hypothesis (`GraphIso/Wielandt/Final.lean`,
  at the thresholds `thrQP 192 181` and the closed solver `qpSolver 192 181`), and their
  answers are correct: `mainSolve_final` (string isomorphism under a group of degree `N`, cost
  at most `genCost N |gs| + 2^{c (λ(N) + 1)⁸}`), and `giSolve_final` and `gi_simpleGraph_final`
  (graphs on `n` points, local cost at most `2^{c (λ(n) + 1)⁸}`, `λ = Nat.log 2`). What
  remains of this item:
  - Wielandt's bound `t < 3 ln n` is not formalized. The transitivity bound used is
    `Tq m = 256 (λ(m) + 1)²`, with milestone 14's analysis generalized to it (`QPHypJ`,
    `qpHypJ_final`); the exponent `8` is unchanged (`PLAN-16.md` §1);
  - the constants `c` are not given explicit values;
  - the entries take valid data (graph data or simple graphs); no checked entry validates
    arbitrary input.
- **Luks's graph isomorphism for bounded valence** needs the reduction from graphs to string
  isomorphism under a group in `Γ_d`. For maximum degree three (milestone 4,
  `GraphIso/Graph/`) the groups are `2`-groups and the reduction is formalized: `gi3_checked`
  costs at most `17500 (2n + 1)²⁶` on connected graphs, and `gi3_all_checked` (milestone 5,
  by components) at most `5100 (2n + 1)²⁸` on all graphs. Up to valence five (milestone 7,
  `GraphIso/Valence/`) the reduction is formalized for maximum degree `Δ`, and its layer groups
  are solvable `{2, 3}`-groups, proved from the construction (the kernel of each restriction
  embeds in a product of permutation groups of at most four twins): `gi5_checked` costs at
  most `28600 (2n + 2)²²²` on connected graphs and `gi5_all_checked` at most
  `7200 (2n + 2)²²⁴` on all graphs. The layer theory (extension criterion, lifts, swap
  criterion) holds for every `Δ`. For every bounded valence (milestone 9) the layer groups have
  simple sections of order at most `max 2 (Δ − 1)!` (`A₀` is a `2`-group, and the kernels of
  the restrictions have sections of order at most `(Δ − 1)!`), and the nonsolvable
  Babai–Cameron–Pálfy theorem (milestone 8) gives `gi_bounded_checked` and
  `gi_bounded_all_checked`, polynomial in `n` for each fixed `Δ` with an enormous exponent.

## Refinements of the backend

- **Stored chains.** Every `stabilizer` call rebuilds a table: `O(n⁶ + L n²)` each time.
  Keeping the table as backend data would make `stabilizer` and `kernel` a base change. This
  would change `D` and `ssDV`, carrying the table invariant through every output group.
- **A cheaper construction.** The worklist checks every pair of entries, for `O(n⁶)`.
  Schreier generators with sifting, or randomized Schreier–Sims with a verification step,
  have better bounds.
- **Smaller generating sets.** Jerrum's filter gives at most `n − 1` generators, against
  `n²` here, which shrinks the `n (s + 1)` reconstruction input of the solver.
- **`cosetReps`** tests each new product against all found representatives (`O(R²)`
  sifts). A transversal read off a table for a base adapted to `K` would avoid the quadratic
  factor.

## Cost-model work

- **Machine time** is done (milestone 17, `GraphIso/TM/`). Naturals are unit-cost words in the
  cost model, and persistent array updates copy (`arrayUpdate`). The compiler stores values in
  cells of a heap, numbers in binary, and `comp_sat` (`GraphIso/TM/Compile.lean`) runs the
  compiled code of a well-formed program within a bound in its local cost and the heap's
  length. `main_exec` and `main_tm` (`GraphIso/TM/Main.lean`) put the program on mathlib's
  Turing machines (`Turing.FinTM2`): for a well-formed entry over the empty signature, a run
  of local cost `C` from an input of length `L` to an output of length `L'` takes at most
  `(κ (C + L + L' + 1))^120 + 1` steps, `κ` depending only on the program. The output's length
  cannot be dropped from a bound of a degree independent of the program: values share cells, so
  a cost of about `m` can build an output of `m²` symbols (`PLAN-17.md` §10.3). The bit lengths
  of intermediate values are bounded inside the proof by the local cost and the input's length
  (`bB`, `fB`), so no separate bounded-word model is needed. Graph isomorphism on Turing
  machines: `gi_tm` and `gi_coset_tm` (`GraphIso/TM/GI.lean`), within `2^{c (λ(L) + 1)⁸}` steps
  on inputs of length `L`; in the number of vertices `n`, `gi_tm_vertices` and
  `gi_coset_tm_vertices` (`GraphIso/TM/Vertices.lean`), within `2^{c (λ(n) + 1)⁸}`.
- **Single-tape machines** are done (milestone 18, `GraphIso/TM/SingleTape/`). `compile_outputs`
  (`Compile.lean`) transfers every `FinTM2` with finite stack alphabets to a finite single-tape
  machine (mathlib's `TM0`, finitely many states and symbols): a run of `T` steps from the input
  `x` to the output `y` becomes at most `compileConst tm (|x| + T + 1)²` transitions from the
  framed input tape `encodeIn x` to the framed output tape `encodeOut y`, input preparation and
  output cleanup included. Graph isomorphism on a single tape: `gi_tm0_vertices` and
  `gi_coset_tm0_vertices` (`GraphIso/TM/SingleTape/GI.lean`), within `2^{c (λ(n) + 1)⁸}`
  transitions on graphs with `n` vertices, and `gi_tm0`, `gi_coset_tm0` in the input tape's
  length `L₀ = L + 2`.
- What remains:
  - `eval` is fuel-bounded, and termination gives only the existence of enough fuel: a public
    runner of `eval` with a proved fuel bound is not provided (the machines of milestone 17
    halt within their proved time);
  - `solveChecked` checks the alphabet bound, but the bound enters no cost statement.
