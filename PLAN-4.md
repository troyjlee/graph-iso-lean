# Lean development plan, milestone 4: isomorphism of graphs of maximum degree three

Prepared 19 October 2026. This continues `PLAN-3.md` (Luks's strategy, commit 9d82a6f). The
conventions, cost model, contracts and standards of `PLAN.md` and `lean/docs/` remain in force.

## Objective and completion criteria

Milestone 3 proved string isomorphism polynomial for nilpotent groups
(`solve_luks_nilpotent`). This milestone turns it into the project's first **graph**
isomorphism theorem. It follows Luks's polynomial-time test for trivalent graphs, for
connected graphs of maximum degree at most three.

**The semantic object.** Let `Z = X ⊔ Y` be the disjoint union on `2n` vertices. Root it at
an edge `e = {a, b}` of `X` and an edge `e' = {a', b'}` of `Y`:

- `V₀ = {a, b, a', b'}`, and level `r` is the distance from `V₀`;
- `Z_r` has the vertices of level `≤ r`, and the edges with an endpoint of level `< r`, plus
  `e` and `e'`;
- `A_r` is the group of permutations of the `2n` points that are supported on level `≤ r`,
  preserve `V₀` and are automorphisms of `Z_r`.

Then:

- `A₀` is the dihedral group of order 8 on `V₀`.
- Every `A_r` is a `2`-group. Restricting `A_{r+1}` to level `≤ r` lands in `A_r`. Its
  kernel only exchanges *twins*, new vertices with the same neighbours in level `r`. A vertex
  of level `r` has at most two neighbours in level `r + 1`, so the kernel has exponent 2.
- `X ≅ Y` by an isomorphism sending `e` to `e'` exactly when `A_{2n}` contains an element
  exchanging `e` and `e'`. One direction needs `X` and `Y` connected. Such an element exists
  exactly when some generator is one, because the elements fixing `{a, b}` form a subgroup.

**One layer as string isomorphism.** Let `L_r` be level `r`, and let `S_r` be the nonempty
subsets of `L_r` with at most three elements.

- The *layer string* on `S_r` records, for each subset `T`:
  - how many new vertices have neighbourhood `T` in `L_r` (at most 2);
  - whether `T` is an edge inside `L_r`.
- An element of `A_r` extends to `A_{r+1}` exactly when it preserves the layer string.
- Run the string-isomorphism solver on the domain `Fin 2n ⊔ S_r`, with the induced action of
  `A_r`, window `S_r`, and the layer string as both `x` and `y`. It returns the extendable
  subgroup `H`, read back on the first `2n` points.
- Then `A_{r+1}` is generated by:
  - the *canonical lifts* of the generators of `H`, matching twins by rank (a homomorphism);
  - the *twin swaps*.

The milestone is complete when:

1. **Layers.** The following are proved for any graph data of maximum degree `≤ 3`:
   - BFS levels by relaxation to a fixpoint, characterized by three properties (level 0 is
     `V₀`, adjacent levels differ by at most 1, each positive level has a witness one level
     down);
   - `A₀` is dihedral of order 8;
   - **the extension criterion**;
   - `A_{r+1} = L(H) ⊔ ⟨twin swaps⟩`;
   - `A_r` is a `2`-group;
   - **the swap criterion**.
2. **Programs.** The following run in the cost language within explicit polynomial costs:
   - levels, subsets, the layer string, the induced generators and their read-back;
   - lifts, twin swaps, the swap test, input validation (adjacency symmetric and without
     loops, maximum degree `≤ 3`, connectivity).

   The induced action is a homomorphism on the setwise stabilizer of `L_r` (`Ind`), with
   restriction to the first `2n` points as a left inverse.
3. **The algorithm.** `gi3Prog` runs inside the procedure `luksBody`. For each edge `e'` of
   `Y`, it computes `A_{2n}` layer by layer, each layer calling the string-isomorphism entry
   and `generate`, and tests the generators. It returns an isomorphism or `none`.
4. **Concrete theorem** with the Schreier–Sims backend, with no assumed oracle:
   - `gi3_spec`: for connected graphs `X`, `Y` on `n` vertices of maximum degree `≤ 3`, the
     result is `some φ` with `φ` an isomorphism, or `none` exactly when none exists;
   - the cost is at most an explicit polynomial in `n`;
   - a validating entry on every input;
   - the statement is related to mathlib's `SimpleGraph` isomorphisms.
5. **Checks.** Small graphs against brute force over all `n!` permutations, as many as the
   interpreter allows: `K₄`, `K₃,₃` against the prism, small trees and paths.
6. **Audit and docs.**

## Design notes

- **Costs come from milestone 3.** Each layer's group is a homomorphic image of a `2`-group,
  hence nilpotent, so `luksEntry_spec` with `ssLuksBounds` and `primBounded_of_isNilpotent`
  bounds each string-isomorphism call.
  - The domain has at most `2n + (2n)³` points.
  - The answer's generators are compressed by `generate` at degree `2n`, keeping at most
    `4n²` generators per layer.
  - There are `2n` layers per edge of `Y`, so the total is polynomial in `n`, with a large
    exponent.
- **Subsets** are sorted lists of length 1–3, indexed through an array keyed by the padded
  triple, so the induced action costs `O(|S_r|)` per generator.
- **No relabelling.** Groups are permutations of `Fin 2n` supported on level `≤ r`. The lift
  of `h` is `h` on level `≤ r`, rank matching on level `r + 1`, and the identity elsewhere.
- **No partial isomorphisms.** Working with automorphisms of `Z` avoids coset
  representatives between two graphs.

## Phases

| Phase | Work | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Plan; executable prototype of the layer algorithm with a brute-force stabilizer | On small graphs: the `A_{2n}` orders and the decisions agree with brute force |
| 1 | Graph data, levels, connectivity, isomorphisms preserve levels | Level characterization; reachability for connected graphs |
| 2 | Layer groups: extension criterion, lifts, twins, `2`-groups, swap criterion | `A_{r+1} = L(H) ⊔ K`; `IsPGroup 2`; the swap criterion |
| 3 | Subset domain, `Ind`, programs and costs | `RunsWithin` for every component; `Ind` a homomorphism |
| 4 | The algorithm and its invariant | Correctness by induction over layers and edges; cost |
| 5 | Schreier–Sims instance, validation, closed form, `SimpleGraph` statement | `gi3_spec`, the checked entry |
| 6 | Checks, audit, documentation | Small graphs, axiom listing, docs |

## Layout

New modules under `lean/GraphIso/Graph/`:

- `Basic.lean`, `Levels.lean` (phase 1);
- `Layers.lean`, `Lift.lean`, `TwoGroup.lean` (phase 2);
- `Subsets.lean` and the `*Prog.lean` files (phase 3);
- `Algorithm.lean` (phase 4).

The Schreier–Sims instance goes in `lean/GraphIso/Solver/Graph.lean`, and checks in
`lean/GraphIsoChecks/Graph.lean`.
