# Graph isomorphism in quasipolynomial time, in Lean

A Lean 4 and Mathlib formalization of Babai's quasipolynomial-time algorithm for graph
isomorphism, without the classification of finite simple groups, ending in a statement about
single-tape Turing machines. The algorithm, its correctness and its running time are proved in
Lean, and the machine that runs it is built from the formalized algorithm.

The statements of record are in [`lean/Challenge.lean`](lean/Challenge.lean). They use only
Mathlib's definitions and a few encoding functions, all in that file (about 150 lines).
[`lean/Solution.lean`](lean/Solution.lean) proves them from the library `GraphIso`, and
[`lean/comparator.json`](lean/comparator.json) lists them for
[Comparator](https://github.com/leanprover/comparator), which checks that the solution proves
exactly the challenge's statements using only the axioms `propext`, `Quot.sound` and
`Classical.choice`. Fuller notes for readers of the development are in
[`lean/docs/formalization-notes.md`](lean/docs/formalization-notes.md).

## The theorems

The machine model is Mathlib's `Turing.TM0`: one tape, infinite in both directions, a finite tape
alphabet `Γ` with a blank symbol, finitely many states, and a transition function that reads the
symbol under the head and either halts or moves to a new state, writing a symbol or moving the head
one cell. A machine starts in its start state with the head on the first symbol of its input and
blanks elsewhere. Time is the number of transitions; halting (the transition function returning
nothing) costs nothing.

Inputs and outputs are words over a small set of symbols `Sym` (binary digits, an end-of-number
mark, list delimiters `cons` and `nil`, a left and a right mark, and `yes` and `no`), which a fixed
injective map `ι` writes into the machine's alphabet, never as the blank:

* a natural number is its binary digits, least significant first, without trailing zeros
  (Mathlib's `Nat.bits`), then the end mark;
* a list is each element after `cons`, then `nil`;
* a simple graph on `Fin n` is the list of the neighbour lists, each in increasing order, of the
  vertices `n - 1`, …, `0`;
* the input of a pair of graphs `G`, `H` on `Fin n` is `n`, `G`, `H` between the marks.

Write `λ(n) = ⌊log₂ n⌋` (with `λ(0) = 0`), so that `2 ^ (c (λ(n) + 1) ^ 8)` is quasipolynomial in
`n`: it is `2 ^ O((log n) ^ 8)`, which no fixed polynomial bounds and which is much smaller than
the `n!` of brute force.

1. **`gi_singleTape` (decision).** There are a `TM0` machine `M` with finitely many symbols and
   states, an injective map `ι` of the symbols into its alphabet, none to the blank, and a
   constant `c`, such that for every `n` and all simple graphs `G`, `H` on `Fin n`, started on
   `ι (input G H)`, `M` makes at most `2 ^ (c (λ(n) + 1) ^ 8)` transitions and halts with the tape
   `ι` of `yes` or `no` between the marks, and the answer is `yes` exactly when `G` and `H` are
   isomorphic.
2. **`gi_coset_singleTape` (all isomorphisms).** There are a `TM0` machine with finitely many
   symbols and states, two injective maps `ιin`, `ιout` of the symbols into its alphabet (it reads
   input symbols and writes output symbols), none to the blank, and a constant `c`, such that for
   every `n` and all simple graphs `G`, `H` on `Fin n` there is an answer `r` with:
   `r = none` exactly when `G` and `H` are not isomorphic; if `r = some (τ, gs)`, a permutation
   `φ` of `Fin n` underlies an isomorphism `G ≃ H` exactly when `τ⁻¹ φ` is in the subgroup
   generated by the list `gs` (so the isomorphisms form the left coset `τ ⟨gs⟩`, and `τ` is one
   of them); and the machine started on `ιin (input G H)` makes at most
   `2 ^ (c (λ(n) + 1) ^ 8)` transitions and halts with the tape `ιout (cosetWord r)`. Here
   `cosetWord none` is `no` between the marks, and `cosetWord (some (τ, gs))` is `yes`, then `τ`
   and the list `gs`, between the marks; a permutation is written as the list of its images of
   `n - 1`, …, `0`, then the list of its inverse images in the same order.
3. **`cosetWord_injective`.** `cosetWord` is injective, so the final tape of the second machine
   determines its answer `r`.

In both theorems the machine, the maps and the constant are chosen before the graphs: one machine
works for all inputs.

## What is not claimed

* **Malformed inputs.** Nothing is said about the machines on words that are not of the form
  `input G H`.
* **The exponent and the constant.** The exponent `8` comes from this formalization's accounting
  and is not optimized. Helfgott, Bajpai and Dona obtain `exp(O((log n) ^ 3))`, with an analysis
  that uses the classification of finite simple groups (Cameron and Maróti's theorem on
  primitive groups). The classification-free route followed here (Pyber's lemma and Babai's
  §13.1) gives a weaker quasipolynomial bound, whose exponent the sources do not compute. The
  constant `c` exists but is not computed.
* **Canonical forms.** Babai's later canonization result is not formalized.
* **Other models.** The statements are about Mathlib's single-tape `TM0` machines and the fixed
  encodings above. The library also proves the bounds for programs in a cost model and for
  Mathlib's multi-stack machines `TM2` (see the notes).

## How the proof goes

The proof is a chain of theorems in the library `GraphIso`, each in its own milestone
(`PLAN.md`, `PLAN-2.md`, …, `PLAN-19.md` record the plans, and [`lean/docs`](lean/docs) the
results):

1. **The algorithm in a cost model (milestones 1–16).** Programs in a small language with
   explicit costs, whose cost is read off an executed semantics, implement Luks's framework for
   string isomorphism under a permutation group, Schreier–Sims as the group backend, and Babai's
   recursion: Weisfeiler–Leman refinement and the Design Lemma, Split-or-Johnson (with Babai's
   2017 correction), the alternating-quotient test, local certificates and their aggregation,
   and the recursion's potential. The classification of finite simple groups is replaced by
   Pyber's elementary bound and an elementary transitivity bound. Milestone 16 proves, with no
   hypothesis, that on every pair of graphs on `Fin n` the entry returns the coset of all
   isomorphisms within local cost `2 ^ (c (λ(n) + 1) ^ 8)`.
2. **Turing machines with stacks (milestone 17).** The cost language is compiled to Mathlib's
   multi-stack machines `TM2`, with a number of steps polynomial in the cost, so the two entries
   run on such machines within `2 ^ (c (λ(L) + 1) ^ 8)` steps in the input's length `L`.
3. **One tape (milestone 18).** Machines with finite stack alphabets are compiled to single-tape
   `TM0` machines with a quadratic overhead, and the bounds are stated in the number of vertices.
4. **The statements of record (milestone 19).** The encodings above are proved equal to the
   library's serializations, and the theorems of milestone 18 give the three statements
   (`lean/GraphIso/Palomar/Bridge.lean`).

  [arXiv:1512.03547](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03547), and *Fixing the UPCC case of
  Split-or-Johnson* (note, January 2017,
  <https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~laci/upcc-fix.pdf>): the algorithm, adapted.
* Harald Andrés Helfgott, with Jitendra Bajpai and Daniele Dona, *Graph isomorphisms in
  quasi-polynomial time*, [arXiv:1710.04574](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.04574): the exposition
  followed for many of the steps.

  [arXiv:1605.08266](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08266): the bound that replaces the
  classification of finite simple groups.
* Eugene M. Luks, *Isomorphism of graphs of bounded valence can be tested in polynomial time*,
  J. Comput. System Sci. 25 (1982): the framework of the earlier milestones.

## Related formalizations

The Theo project reports a separate Lean formalization of Babai's result in
[*Beyond the Library: An Agentic Framework for Autoformalizing Research Mathematics*](https://openreview.net/forum?id=XF8jQ5EnrA),
§3.2.9 and Appendix C.10. It covers String Isomorphism, including search, and derives Graph
Isomorphism and Coset Intersection, with quasipolynomial RAM time measured in encoded input
length. Its public source declares the five cited group-theoretic assumptions listed in the
paper, beyond Lean's standard axioms. This submission proves the graph decision and coset-output
statements on a single-tape machine, with a bound in vertex count and only `propext`,
`Quot.sound` and `Classical.choice`, through a classification-free route. The formalization notes give the
comparison and its verification limits. We make no priority claim.

## How it was made

The development took place from 18 to 29 October 2026. The Lean code, the plans and the initial
documentation were written by an AI agent (Claude Code, with Claude Opus 5.5), directed
by the human author, who set the goals, made the decisions recorded in the plans and approved
the plans, including the statements above. Milestones 1–6 were reviewed by OpenAI's Codex
(gpt-6-astra at maximum effort), with the author passing the messages between the agents by
hand. From milestone 7 on, every stage ran in a write–audit loop: a mechanical gate (a full
build with warnings as errors, an axiom audit of every declaration, banned constructs such as
`sorry` and `native_decide`, and a snapshot of protected theorem statements that may not change
without the author's approval), then an adversarial audit by OpenAI's Codex (gpt-6-astra at
maximum effort), which could read the repository and run its own Lean probes, for up to three
rounds. OpenAI Codex later repaired the snapshot tooling and revised the submission
documentation and metadata. The audit reports are not published; the notes summarize the
process. The Lean proofs were not reviewed line by line by a person: their correctness rests on
Lean's kernel and the independent checkers that Comparator runs, and their meaning on the
statement file.

## Building and checking

The Lean project is in `lean/` (toolchain `leanprover/lean4:v4.35.0-rc2`, Mathlib pinned in
`lean/lake-manifest.json`).

```text
cd lean
lake exe cache get        # Mathlib's prebuilt files
lake build                # the library GraphIso and Solution
lake build Challenge      # the statements; the only warnings are the three deliberate `sorry`s
lake comparator           # Linux with bubblewrap; checks Solution against Challenge
```

The library's own checks (`lake build GraphIsoChecks`, about two hours, some modules needing
20–40 GB of memory; see `lean/docs/conventions.md`) include concrete kernel-checked runs and an
axiom audit of every declaration (`lean/GraphIsoChecks/AxiomAudit.lean`).
